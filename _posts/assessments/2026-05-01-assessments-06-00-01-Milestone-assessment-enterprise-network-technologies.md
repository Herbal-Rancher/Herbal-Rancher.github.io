## Overview

This milestone assessment examines the enterprise network technologies required to provide secure, reliable, and scalable connectivity across headquarters, branch offices, remote employees, and cloud-hosted business services. The assessment connects LANs, WLANs, enterprise switching, VLANs, WANs, VPNs, and cloud networking with the performance, scalability, security, and reliability requirements of a large-scale business environment.

The enterprise design also demonstrates how switching features, common network ports and protocols, transmission media and transceivers, and network topologies and architectures work together to support efficient operations and business continuity.

---

## Assessment Focus

* LAN and WLAN connectivity
* Enterprise switching technologies and features
* VLAN segmentation and 802.1Q trunking
* Layer 3 routing and inter-VLAN communication
* WAN connectivity between enterprise locations
* Site-to-site and remote-access VPN connectivity
* Cloud networking and hybrid infrastructure
* Network performance, scalability, security, and reliability
* RSTP and LACP redundancy
* 802.1X, port security, ACLs, and QoS
* Common enterprise ports, protocols, services, and traffic types
* TCP and UDP transport characteristics
* Copper, multimode fiber, single-mode fiber, and wireless transmission media
* SFP, SFP+, and QSFP-family transceivers
* Star, partial mesh, hub-and-spoke, point-to-point, and spine-leaf topologies
* Hierarchical, collapsed-core, client-server, and hybrid cloud architectures
* Business continuity and operational efficiency

## Reflection

This milestone assessment strengthened my understanding of enterprise networking as an integrated architecture rather than a collection of separate technologies. Evaluating LANs, WLANs, switching, VLANs, WANs, VPNs, and cloud networking showed how each technology contributes differently to performance, scalability, security, and reliability.

The assessment also helped me connect logical network design with the technologies that support it at each layer, including switching features, network protocols and services, transmission media, transceivers, topologies, redundancy, and security controls. Most importantly, I reinforced how these technologies work together to maintain access to critical applications, communications, and data as an enterprise grows and when individual network components or connections fail.
