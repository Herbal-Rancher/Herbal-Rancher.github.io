## Overview

A Cisco Packet Tracer simulation lab examining TCP and UDP communications, multiplexing, application ports, and packet exchanges. By generating HTTP, FTP, DNS, and email traffic, I observed how protocols behave and how their PDUs move across the network.

---

## Assessment Focus

- Generate and observe network traffic in Packet Tracer Simulation Mode
- Examine multiplexing and identify protocols using PDU event colors
- Trace TCP's three-way handshake and reliable communication
- Interpret TCP source and destination ports, sequence numbers, acknowledgment numbers, and flags
- Compare TCP-based HTTP, FTP, and email traffic with UDP-based DNS traffic
- Identify application services and ports, including HTTP (80), FTP (21), DNS (53), SMTP (25), and POP3 (110)
- Use Capture/Forward, simulation filters, and inbound/outbound PDU details to analyze network communications

## Reflection

I selected Lab 43 to strengthen my understanding of TCP and UDP communications and their roles in network operations and troubleshooting. My primary challenge was interpreting TCP sequence numbers, acknowledgment numbers, and control flags. Packet Tracer's Simulation Mode helped me visualize the TCP three-way handshake and examine how different applications exchange data.

This lab reinforced my understanding of TCP reliability, UDP's connectionless operation, multiplexing, and application port numbers. Analyzing HTTP, FTP, DNS, and email traffic improved my ability to interpret packet headers, recognize protocol behavior, and troubleshoot network communications.

**My key takeaway:** Understanding TCP and UDP packet exchanges is essential for effective network analysis, troubleshooting, and reliable communication.
